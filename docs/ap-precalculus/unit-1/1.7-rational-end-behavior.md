## Core Concepts & Learning Objectives

- **Horizontal Asymptotes:** Governed by comparing numerator degree $n$ and denominator degree $m$ of $R(x) = \dfrac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$:
- - $n < m$: Horizontal asymptote at $y = 0$.
- - $n = m$: Horizontal asymptote at $y = \dfrac{a_n}{b_m}$ (ratio of leading coefficients).
- - $n > m$: No horizontal asymptote.
- **Slant (Oblique) Asymptotes:** Occur when numerator degree is exactly one higher than denominator degree ($n = m + 1$). Found via polynomial division: $R(x) = (mx + b) + \dfrac{r(x)}{Q(x)}$.

---

## Essential Formulas & Definitions

**Horizontal Asymptote Limit Definition:**

$$\lim_{x \to \infty} R(x) = L \quad \text{or} \quad \lim_{x \to -\infty} R(x) = L \implies y = L \text{ is a horizontal asymptote}$$

!!! tip "AP Exam Tip"
    A rational function can intersect its horizontal asymptote for small values of $x$. Horizontal asymptotes describe only global end behavior as $x \to \pm\infty$, unlike vertical asymptotes which can never be crossed.
